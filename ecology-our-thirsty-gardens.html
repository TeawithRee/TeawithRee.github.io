Our Thirsty Gardens

The garden is thirsty this week. The soil is cracking a little at the edges, and the leaves seem to lean towards the tap.

On Sunday 22 November, the UN climate talks in Antalya, COP31, closed without an agreement. Countries could not agree on how to move away from fossil fuels.

Closer to home, Cape Town's Level 1 water restrictions begin on 1 December. Gardens may be watered with municipal water on Tuesdays and Saturdays only, for one hour, before 9am or after 6pm.

The big decisions happen far away, in rooms most of us will never sit in. Water is closer. We hold it in our hands every day.

Perhaps this summer is a chance to reset how we use it. A bucket in the shower while the water warms. Rinse water carried out to the roses. Mulch spread over the beds to keep the soil cool. Watering early, while the kettle boils for the first cup of tea.

None of this asks us to be perfect. Each of us will find what fits our own home, our own garden and our own way of living.

The trees outside still catch the morning light. They have lived through dry summers before. Their roots reach deep, and they wait for rain.

We can learn a little from them. Use what we have with care. Notice what we're given. Trust that small choices, made in many homes, add up.

What will you try in your garden this summer?
