Modern Tools

Technology that serves thought, not the other way round.

When Technology Has to Explain Itself

⋮

I read something this morning that made me put my cup down.

AI as a Creative Companion

⋮

I think technology should feel like an extension of what you actually want to do, not a replacement for thinking. The best tools are the ones that get out of your way and let you think more clearly, create more freely and connect more deeply.

The Rise of Digital Rituals

⋮

We think of rituals as ancient. Candles. Chants. Offerings. But the human need for ritual is not bound to any technology. It is bound to intention.

Abstract Tech: Visual Language of the Future

⋮

Technology has developed its own aesthetic. Neon grids. Glitch effects. Holographic interfaces. These visuals are not just decoration. They are a visual language that signals innovation, futurity and the dissolution of boundaries between physical and digital.

Building a Second Brain

⋮

Your mind is for having ideas, not holding them. That is the idea behind the second brain movement and honestly, it has changed how I think. Externalise your thinking so that your biological brain is free to do what it does best: connect, create and wonder.

What AI Can Give Back to a Human Day

⋮

A personal reflection on using AI to reduce friction without giving away judgement, privacy or the pleasure of doing things slowly.

The Difference Between Assistance and Dependence

⋮

A quiet examination of how useful tools can support a person without taking over their attention, judgement or agency.

What Responsible AI Means on an Ordinary Day

⋮

The questions I ask about AI are ordinary ones: privacy, trust, responsibility and whether a useful tool gives us time back.

The Future Should Still Have Room for People

⋮

I am interested in technology when it brings people into better conversations, not when it tries to make the people unnecessary. A tool can organise a meeting, find a pattern, or help with a first draft. It cannot decide what matters to the people sitting around the table.

A Tool Is Only as Wise as the Question

⋮

There is something almost ritual about setting a cup of tea down and looking out at the small rectangle of sky my kitchen window offers. It is an ordinary pause, but it reminds me that tools are best judged by the company they keep.

Can AI Help the Climate Without Becoming Another Cost?

⋮

Lately I have been thinking about the neat, clever tools that promise to help us meet climate goals. They can do startling things with data, spot patterns we would otherwise miss and suggest where resources might be stretched further.

Can Technology Help Us Notice More?

⋮

I have a small ritual of standing at the kitchen sink for a minute before I turn on the kettle, partly because it feels like an odd kind of permission to just be. Lately I have been aware of how much the tools in my pocket and on my wrist shape that minute and whether they help me notice the things I want to notice.

The Cost of Convenience

⋮

I make a cup of tea, the kettle humming on its own and a notification tells me my shopping will arrive in an hour. There is a small comfort in that certainty. It means no trolley-wrangling in the rain, no rummaging for loose change at the shop counter and no need to plan the day around opening hours.

When a Recommendation Becomes a Narrowing

⋮

There is a peculiar economy to suggestions. One small nudge on my screen and the afternoon seems to rearrange itself, as if the app, the playlist or the online shop has quietly decided what I ought to like next.

The Infrastructure We Forget

⋮

I was making a pot of tea the other morning and noticed the kettle click off before I had time to breathe. It felt like a small miracle, which of course it is when you think about the wire and metal and company of engineers that make that single familiar sound possible.
