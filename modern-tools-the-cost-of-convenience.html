The Cost of Convenience

Logistics research comparing delivery speeds has consistently found that same day and next day delivery carry a measurably higher carbon cost per item than slower, consolidated shipping. Smaller loads, less efficient routing, more vehicles making more separate trips to hit a tighter window.

The convenience is real. So is the bill, it's just addressed to somewhere the customer never sees.

I make a cup of tea, the kettle humming on its own. A notification tells me my shopping will arrive in an hour.

I used to treat that speed as pure gain, time saved with no obvious cost attached anywhere. Understanding what the faster delivery actually requires, more trucks, less efficient loads, changed how automatically I reach for the fastest option available.

I still use it sometimes. I've just stopped pretending the convenience was free, when what actually happened was the cost got moved somewhere quieter, onto a road, a driver's schedule, an emissions column nobody hands me the receipt for.

I'm not giving up the fast delivery entirely. I'm just done pretending the speed arrived from nowhere, with nothing on the other end of it actually paying for the difference in real vans and real hours.

The receipt for that speed was always going out to someone. I'd just never been shown it before.

I'm trying to notice the receipt more often now, even when nobody hands it to me directly at the door. The kettle finishes before the thought does most mornings.

I'm trying to let the thought catch up before I tap confirm on the option that arrives soonest.
