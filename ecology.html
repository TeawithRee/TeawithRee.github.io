Eco-Tips for Everyday Living

⋮

Sustainability is not a lifestyle brand. It is a collection of small, sometimes inconvenient choices that somehow add up to a life you can stand behind. You do not need a bamboo toothbrush to begin. You only need a little curiosity and the willingness to ask why you do things the way you do.

A Garden Is a Small Climate Conversation

⋮

A garden does not have to be large to change the way we look at the world. A few pots on a balcony can teach us about heat, water, soil and patience.

A child I know cupped a duckling in his hands last week and pressed his forehead to its head. I have not stopped thinking about his face.

Oceans in Focus: A Visual Essay

⋮

The ocean does not care about our schedules. It moves to tides that were set in motion before the first human drew breath. That is what makes it humbling. Also terrifying, if I am honest. And that is what makes it necessary.

What Trees Give Us Before We Notice

⋮

There are trees I know by the way they change the light. I walk past them without always looking up, but I notice when one has been cut down. The pavement feels hotter. The street sounds harder. Something has disappeared before I have found the words for it.

Sustainable Living When the Forecast Is a Warning

⋮

I used to think sustainable living was mostly a private discipline — the bin sorted properly, the water used carefully, a way of being tidy with the planet the way you might be tidy with a kitchen. Now I think it has to answer to something larger than tidiness, because the forecast has started making that unavoidable.

A World Made Smaller by Birds

⋮

I was making a cup of tea the other morning when a flash of white and black cut across the grey sky and for a few minutes the garden felt as if it were linked by invisible thread to somewhere far beyond the rooftops.

Conservation Begins with Attention

⋮

There is something modest and stubborn about attention. It does not demand grand gestures or specialist knowledge. It is the act of turning your head, slowing your pace and noticing what is already happening around you.

Rainforest After Rain

⋮

There is something about the moment right after the rain stops that always makes me slow down — the way the air changes and the forest seems to have a new patience. I find myself listening before I look, because the sounds tell you more than the shapes.

The Air We Share

⋮

I often notice the air before I see anything else. On some mornings it comes cool and sharp, carrying the faint yeast of a baker’s van and the metallic tang of the river. On others it is heavy with the soft, sweet smoke of a neighbour’s wood burner and the dust of last night’s traffic.

What We Owe the Soil

⋮

I think about soil more often than most might expect. There is a certain humility in watching a handful of earth crumble between your fingers and recognising that most of what supports us is quiet, dark and underfoot.
