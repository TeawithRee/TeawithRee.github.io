What GLP-1 Actually Means and What My Own Sugar Habit Taught Me

GLP-1 isn't a drug. It's a hormone your own gut produces naturally after you eat, one job among several being to signal your brain that you've had enough and to help regulate insulin at the same time.

The medications built around it are synthetic versions designed to mimic that signal and make it last far longer than your own body normally would. I used to hear GLP-1 mentioned as though it were a brand rather than a hormone, another word in the long list of things wellness culture argues about without anyone quite agreeing on the actual biology underneath.

My own sugar habit taught me something adjacent to that confusion. For years I treated a craving late at night as a character flaw, evidence I lacked whatever discipline other people apparently had in abundance.

Understanding that appetite runs on hormones I don't consciously control changed the shame more than it changed the craving itself. The craving is still there some evenings.

What's different is I no longer read it as a verdict on who I am. None of this makes the craving disappear on command.

It just means I'm arguing with a hormone now instead of a character flaw, which turns out to be a much fairer fight to actually have. Understanding the biology didn't hand me a fix.

It handed me a more honest opponent, one I can actually reason with instead of just quietly resenting. Biology isn't an excuse.

It's just a more accurate description of what was actually happening, underneath a story about willpower that was never quite telling the truth.
